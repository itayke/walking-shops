Pixel-art takes on the shops, walk-ins and parachuters, drawn by code the way the game's renderer would draw them: a few shop parts plus a player tint, on a fixed grid of art pixels per column. Nothing here changes the game.

Legs

Four ways to walk. Each shop stands up, steps one column, sits, and walks back. Legs keep their length: standing up and sitting down bend the knees out sideways instead of stretching the legs. Pick one in the toolbar to see it on the street and in the states above.

Grid and sign text

Each column below is 96 screen pixels, which is what a 1080p screen gives a 20-column street. The grid decides how much of a name fits on a sign. These follow the Light setting only.

Decided

  • 32 art pixels per column, daylight, half-pixel sign text. A shop is 27×23 with a 3-pixel roof ledge, and a sign fits about 10 characters per line on two lines, under a row of padding.
  • Rotation and scaling are fine. Lean, dizzy sway, push and topple rotate the drawn shop, and the landing squash scales it, even where tilted pixels leave the grid.
  • No marker over your own shop. Everyone shares one screen; each phone shows its shop's tint instead.
  • Thin ink legs with a side-view stride. The stride runs forward in both directions, and standing up or sitting down bends the knees out sideways in about an eighth of a second.

Still open

  • Keep the scale a whole number. A 96-pixel column is exactly 3×. Street widths that don't divide evenly need letterboxing or uneven pixels, and the zoom-out as towers grow should step between whole scales.
  • Tints for 30 players. Hues snap to 15° steps, which gives 24 three-shade ramps. Thirty players need a second set, such as paler ramps or a different awning pattern.
  • Let stacked shops touch. Each shop fills its whole floor (body plus roof ledge), so towers read as buildings. Today's body is 80% of a floor and leaves a gap above each shop.
  • Draw shops in code and hand-draw only people. Hand-drawn shop sprites would need a copy for every tint and squash frame. People and canopies are small enough to draw as pixel maps.
